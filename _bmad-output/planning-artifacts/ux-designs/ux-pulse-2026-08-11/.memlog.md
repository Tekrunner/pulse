- (event by user) Yann was formerly an economics PhD student focused on climate change and developed familiarity with macroeconomic and climate indicators through frequent reading; after moving into IT, that familiarity faded.
- (decision by user) Pulse should provide quick access to personally selected indicators that Yann considers important for understanding the world.
- (decision by user) The desired experience is to rebuild and maintain familiarity through repeated exposure, not merely support isolated fact lookup.
- (decision by user) Pulse is desktop-primary, with smartphone support for occasional quick lookups.
- (decision by user) Pulse should prioritize rich Reports with multiple purpose-built visuals that expose several aspects of an issue; it must not collapse the experience into summarized dashboards.
- (decision by user) The Homepage primary at-a-glance outcome is knowing whether the expected ingestion pipelines executed and whether they completed without issue.
- (decision by user) The Homepage primary function is browsing and accessing Reports; pipeline health is an important secondary function.
- (decision by user) Homepage health is compact by default, with pipeline and source details available through collapsible UI elements.
- (decision by user) The Homepage Report collection is expected to remain small; no elaborate discovery or taxonomy is needed.
- (decision by user) Each Report listing needs only its name and last-update date.
- (decision by user) Reports can be sorted alphabetically or by last-update date.
- (decision by user) Every Report must expose data date and source/provenance metadata; whether those belong at Report or Visual scope remains unresolved.
- (decision by user) Indicator definitions will probably be associated with individual Visuals rather than the Report as a whole.
- (decision by user) Avoid an ambiguous Report-level 'data date': pipeline execution time, Report generation time, and represented data period are distinct facts.
- (assumption by coach) Likely metadata ownership: exact data period and provenance per Visual; pipeline execution time in Homepage health; Report generation time may supply the Homepage listing's last-update value. Awaiting confirmation.
- (override by user) Homepage health must account for every independently scheduled or executable pipeline, not only one latest overall pipeline run.
- (decision by user) The compact Homepage health view must make it possible to verify that all pipelines executed as expected and are healthy; each pipeline's execution detail is expandable.
- (change by coach) The UX must surface the terminology/model mismatch between the PRD's singular Pipeline run and the user's multiple pipeline executions for resolution in architecture/spec work.
- (assumption by coach) For meaningful report sorting, 'last updated' should mean the last substantive change to data or content used by that Report, not routine regeneration time; exact observation periods remain per Visual. Awaiting user confirmation.
- (decision by user) A Report last-updated date means the last substantive change to data or content used by that Report; routine regeneration with unchanged inputs does not change it. Exact observation periods and provenance remain per Visual.
- (decision by user) In-browser exploration must support adding and removing individual countries and country groups to broaden or narrow comparisons.
- (decision by user) In-browser exploration must support changing the date range between longer historical and shorter present-focused views.
- (decision by user) In-browser exploration must support viewing absolute levels and year-over-year changes, including switching between them or augmenting one with the other when the Visual calls for it.
- (decision by user) In-browser exploration must support overlaying alternate definitions or versions of an indicator, such as GDP variants using different purchasing-power, exchange-rate, and constant/current-price bases.
- (override by user) The country/group selection, date-range changes, absolute-versus-YoY views, and indicator-version overlays are illustrative examples only; they do not define or bound in-browser exploration.
- (decision by user) In-browser exploration remains open-ended and purpose-built: each Report or Visual may expose whatever exploratory questions and interactions its subject requires, without a fixed system-wide capability list.
- (decision by user) Cross-Visual coordination, including shared country or date selections, is defined per Report rather than by a global Pulse rule.
- (decision by user) Each pipeline in the compact Homepage health view shows at least its name, status, and execution date.
- (decision by user) Expanded pipeline detail should expose as much actionable information as possible about what went wrong.
- (assumption by coach) The exact expanded diagnostic fields are deferred until pipeline implementation contracts define the available failure evidence; this is not a UX-finalization blocker.
- (decision by user) Suspect-data warnings must always appear in the affected pipeline's status and expanded detail.
- (decision by user) Affected Reports or Visuals should also show suspect-data warnings when reliable impact traceability is practical.
- (assumption by coach) Architecture should evaluate lineage from pipeline/source through datasets to Reports and Visuals; UX must not claim report-level impact when traceability is unreliable.
- (decision by user) When technically possible, no-row, query-error, and schema-incompatibility states are isolated and explained within the affected Visual while the rest of the Report remains usable.
- (assumption by coach) Failure isolation depends on query and rendering boundaries; a shared duckdb-wasm initialization or data-delivery failure may require a broader Report-level error state.
- (decision by user) On smartphones, the primary requirement is that Report Visuals remain readable; landscape orientation is an acceptable minimum viewing mode.
- (decision by user) Smartphone use is primarily quick indicator checking rather than detailed analysis.
- (decision by user) Advanced dynamic exploration controls are lower priority on smartphones than on desktop.
- (decision by user) Pulse follows common web accessibility standards, explicitly WCAG 2.2 Level AA.
- (decision by coach) Accessibility floor covers keyboard operation, visible unobscured focus, semantic structure and labels, non-color-only state communication, AA contrast, browser zoom/reflow, adequate targets, reduced-motion support, and accessible equivalents for data Visuals.
- (decision by user) Pulse v1 uses English for interface labels, Report prose, and indicator definitions.
- (assumption by coach) Localization is out of scope for v1 unless a later requirement introduces it.
- (decision by user) Pulse uses a dark theme as its default visual theme.
- (decision by user) Motion is restrained and functional rather than decorative.
- (decision by user) Animations are removed or reduced when the operating system requests reduced motion.
