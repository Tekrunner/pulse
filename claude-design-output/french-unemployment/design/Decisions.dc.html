Pulse · French unemployment

Design decisions and rationale

What was decided, why, and what it costs. Everything below becomes binding on approval.

The reading order is the narrative

Headline → slack → age → participation → territory → international. It follows the recorded reading behaviour rather than dataset convenience: a reader arrives with a number in mind, tests it against the wider measures, then looks for themselves, and compares outward last. The territorial and international sections are the two that most reward a second visit, so they sit where a returning reader can reach them by scrolling past a headline they have already read.

The rate and the headcount are two plots, never one

A dual axis would invent a relationship between a percentage and a headcount whose alignment is arbitrary. They are stacked, sharing an x-axis and a quarter, on separate scales. The cost is vertical space; the alternative is a chart that lies about correlation.

The four slack measures are small multiples, not a stack

They overlap — a long-term unemployed person is also unemployed — so they are not parts of a whole and must never be stacked. Their published rates have four different denominators (labour force, labour force, population 15–64, employment), so those rates must never share an axis either. Four panels, each with its own scale, its own headcount and its own rate with the denominator spelled out in words. A standing caution says so in the figure, not in a footnote.

Colour was computed, not chosen

Series colours are the documented categorical order, validated against this report's own surface (#232532) rather than a default: three slots pass all-pairs (worst CVD ΔE 9.4), eight pass adjacent (worst 8.4). An earlier hand-picked palette failed on four of five checks — too light, too grey, and two pairs indistinguishable — which is why the validator ran before anything was drawn.

The map ramp is one hue, five classes, ascending lightness with ascending rate because the surface is dark; it passes monotonicity, a ΔL floor of 0.06 between steps, and 2.29:1 at the recessive end. A six-step version failed the ΔL gate and was cut to five.

Age bands were tried as an ordinal single-hue ramp, which would have encoded their natural order. It failed: the darkest step reached only 1.87:1 against this surface. They use three categorical slots instead, assigned in band order, with every line directly labelled at its end so identity never rests on hue.

Colour follows the country, not its rank

France is fixed in slot 1 and cannot be removed. Every other comparator claims a slot when it is added and releases it only when removed, so taking Spain off the chart never repaints Germany. Eight concurrent series is a hard cap: past eight the palette has no ninth colour a colour-blind reader can separate, so a ninth is refused with an explanation rather than served with a colour that lies. The cost is real — a reader cannot put all 38 members on one chart — and the ranked table is the answer for that question instead.

Gaps are shown, never smoothed

Three separate absences, three deliberate treatments, none of them zero:

  • Mayotte has a boundary but no localised rate: it renders in the not-published fill with a dashed edge, and the legend names that class.
  • The four overseas départements before 2014 switch to the same treatment, with the period control stating why.
  • Switzerland and New Zealand stay in the comparator list and render an explicit "not published monthly" chip. They were not quietly removed.

Periods are stated, never implied

The three data families reach different dates — national Q2 2026, départements Q1 2026, international August 2026 — so every figure carries its own represented period and the header states all three. The map explicitly contrasts its own national reference (7.9% metropolitan France, Q1) with the national headline (8.3%, France including Mayotte, Q2) so the two are never read as a discrepancy.

The international chart draws each country to its own latest month and labels it, rather than cutting every line back to the slowest reporter. The United Kingdom therefore ends about three months short, which the caption explains as the provider's calendar.

Every figure has a text equivalent

Not a summary — the same values. The map's equivalent is the full sortable ranking of all 100 départements; at phone width that table replaces the map outright, because 100 shapes are unreadable at that size. Tooltips never gate a value: direct labels carry the endpoints, the table carries everything, and keyboard focus shows what hover shows.

What is design-only and must not be ported

The map outlines on these artboards are decimated to roughly 30 vertices per département so the review canvas stays light. Production renders the published IGN geometry unchanged. Nothing else here is representative: every number on every artboard was read from the published Parquet.

Open questions for the approver

  • The eight-series cap on the international chart is a judgement. If a reader must compare more, the answer is the ranked table, not more colours — confirm that trade is acceptable.
  • The headline section shows ten years by default. Five would emphasise the recent rise; all 23 would put the 2015 peak of 10.5% on screen. Ten is the compromise.
  • Class breaks on the map are round numbers (6, 7, 8, 10) rather than quintiles, so the legend is memorable. Quintiles would spread the colours more evenly across départements.
