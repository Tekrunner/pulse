Design decisions

Decisions this design takes on the brief's open points, each with its reason. They become binding on approval.

Composition

  • Eight sections in the reading order of the brief: output and growth, demand, branches, income, dollars, OECD standing, drivers, departements. One sticky control row owns the represented period, the selected year and the comparison set.
  • The comparison set is a row of compact chips showing ISO codes, with the full name as tooltip and in the remove button's label; the add menu and the OECD reference toggle take the same form, so the default set fits on one line and each further economy adds a chip, not a row (revised after review: full-height chips with names stacked the row vertically).
  • Pulse dark tokens throughout; France is always the accent teal (#73d6c2), comparators take a local palette, aggregates are grey and dashed or hollow.

Headline and the quarterly tail

  • N = 7½ years: quarters begin at Q1 of the year seven years before the latest quarter's year (2019-Q1 with 2026-Q2 published). It is computed from the data, never a calendar date, and puts the pre-pandemic year and 2020 in quarterly detail.
  • Linear scale for the volume level by default, with evenly spaced round ticks in every window; a switch gives a log scale, on which equal slopes mean equal growth rates. On log, wide ranges get doubling ticks (200, 400, 800, 1 600), which are evenly spaced; narrow ranges get round steps, which a log axis cannot space evenly (revised after review: mixed round ticks on a default log axis looked unevenly spaced, and a 25-year window showed a single tick).
  • The seam is a shaded band with a dashed edge and an in-plot label; the paragraph beneath names the working-day step once, because no mark can say it.
  • Per-inhabitant mode is annual only; the band disappears in that mode.
  • Levels are at constant 2020 prices (chained volumes) in the tiles and the level figure (revised after review). Tiles follow the selected year, each with its own period line; with a year beyond the annual accounts, the annual tiles say where they end.

Demand side

  • Diverging stacked bars with GDP growth as a dot. Households are merged with non-profit institutions and inventories with valuables, both labelled; the merges keep the stack additive and remove two slivers too thin to read.

Branches

  • Small multiples, one panel per branch, one colour, in a fixed alphabetical order so panels never move when the year changes (revised after review: ordering by share reshuffled them). Each panel has its own vertical scale so year-to-year movements of a few tenths of a point stay visible; the panel title carries the level and the change since the window start, so levels are compared by number, not by height. (Revised after review: a stacked area hid small changes and needed too many colours.)
  • Each panel with more than one sub-branch has its own open button; a breadcrumb gives the way back.
  • A88 shows the shorter-span warning whenever it is on screen; residual industries are dashed and tagged "derived".

Income

  • Four panels, one per share of GDP, each on its own scale (revised after review: slow-moving shares were invisible in a 100 % stack). Compensation of employees alone is dashed in the labour panel.

Axes, tiles and money

  • Every plot has a y axis with ticks, an x axis with year ticks and a unit title above the plot.
  • The five tiles follow the selected year; the two quarter tiles show that year's last published quarter.
  • Amounts follow French convention for grouping and currency: thousands separated by a narrow no-break space and the currency after the number — 2 991 bn €, 43 350 €, 64.0 k$. Decimals keep the point, as the page is in English.

Selection beyond a section's edge

  • Selected year defaults to the latest annual year. Sections without that year say so in their strip; the map, which would otherwise be empty for the latest national year, draws its nearest published year and labels it as such in its caption, strip and a banner, with a button to select that year everywhere (revised after review: an all-grey map read as broken).

Dollars

  • Linear dollar level above a stacked bar of the three log-point terms, with the net change as a white tick so the sum is read without arithmetic.

International

  • Levels within a year as ranked bars at current PPP (OECD as a dashed reference bar, never ranked); paths over time as lines at constant 2020 PPP; growth as lines, clipped to ±12/14 points so one outlier year does not flatten the rest.

Drivers

  • France over time as cumulative log-point changes since the window's start (from 1970), four factors plus the total; UN projection years drawn dotted.
  • Across economies, one bar per economy: its gap to France in GDP per inhabitant in log points, split into the four factors, with a white tick for the total (revised after review: the dot plot of ratios to France was hard to read). Aggregates, which have no UN working-age share, show employment per inhabitant unsplit; economies with screened hours are named beneath. A table gives the four factors in their own units.

Departements

  • Eight bins on France = 100 with a neutral 90–110 class, blue below and orange above, unequal widths chosen because half the departements lie between 72 and 89 while Paris exceeds 300.
  • An inset for Paris and the inner ring, and one per overseas departement.
  • Payload: outlines projected and simplified (Douglas–Peucker, 0.45 px at a 560 px map; rings under 1.2 px dropped). The prototype data file is 0.5 MB. Production should apply the same tolerance in the report's row mapping or a declared derived geometry; this is a design decision awaiting approval.

Copy

  • No sentence on the page carries a value, direction or period; every number renders from rows in a tile, strip, label or table.
